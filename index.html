About This Series

The Fastest, Most Urgent Gospel

Mark is the shortest and most action-packed of the four Gospels. Written likely by John Mark (a companion of both Peter and Paul), it moves at a relentless pace. The word "immediately" appears over 40 times. There's no nativity story, no genealogy. Mark throws us straight into the action.

This is a gospel for people who want to cut to the chase. Who is Jesus? What did he actually do? What does it cost to follow him? Mark doesn't let you sit on the fence.

⚡

Urgency

Mark's "immediately" pace calls us to respond now, not someday.

👑

Authority

Jesus commands demons, storms, sickness and death with equal power.

✚

Suffering Servant

The Son of God comes not to be served, but to give his life as a ransom.

🕐

Discipleship

Following Jesus is costly, demanding, and the only path worth taking.

How to Use This Guide

Getting the Most Out of Each Session

Before the Session

Read the passage yourself during the week. Ask God to show you something specific before you arrive.

During the Session

Read the passage aloud together first. Use the questions as a guide, not a checklist. Trust the Spirit to lead the conversation.

Discussion Questions

Each session has four questions: one opener to get everyone talking, two general questions grounded in the text, and one deeper question to push further.

Closing Well

Always end with the "Take It Away" challenge and pray specifically. Don't close with good intentions. Close with God.

The Study Plan

Week 1

Straight Into It

Mark 1:1-20
▼

"The time has come. The kingdom of God has come near. Repent and believe the good news!"

Mark 1:15
Mark wastes no time. There's no Christmas story, no genealogy. In just 20 verses we get John the Baptist, Jesus' baptism, his temptation in the wilderness, and the call of the first four disciples. This series begins with the same question Mark intends to confront us with: not who do we think Jesus is, but who does God say he is, and will we respond?

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: If you had to describe Jesus in one word to a friend who had never heard of him, what would you say?
  • Mark opens by declaring this is the good news of Jesus the Messiah, the Son of God. What has God done in Christ that makes this genuinely good news, regardless of how your life circumstances feel right now?
  • When Jesus calls Simon and Andrew, they drop their nets "immediately." What area of your life is Jesus calling you to respond to with that same urgency, and what is making you hesitate?
Deeper

John says someone greater than him is coming. When was the last time you were genuinely in awe of who Jesus is, not just grateful for what he has done for you?

Activity

Go around the room and each person share: "Before I understood who Jesus really was, I thought he was..." Give everyone 60 seconds. It's a low-pressure opener that naturally flows into the discussion about who Mark says Jesus actually is.

Take It Away This Week

  • Read Mark 1:21-45 before next session and note how many miracles happen and how fast.
  • Identify one area of your life where Jesus is calling you to drop your nets. Write it down and pray over it daily this week.

Prayer Focus

Ask God to give fresh eyes to see Jesus clearly throughout this series. Pray that each person leaves not just informed but genuinely encountered by God through Mark's Gospel.
Week 2

Who Has Authority Like This?

Mark 1:21 - 2:12
▼

"What is this? A new teaching, and with authority! He even gives orders to impure spirits and they obey him."

Mark 1:27
Jesus heals a man with an unclean spirit, cures Simon's mother-in-law, cleanses a leper, and forgives and heals a paralysed man, all in rapid succession. The crowd's question "Who is this?" is the central question of Mark. The religious leaders are already bristling by chapter 2. Jesus claims to forgive sins, which only God can do. His authority isn't just impressive; it's a claim that demands a response.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Have you ever witnessed something that you couldn't fully explain, medically, circumstantially, or spiritually? What was it?
  • The crowd is amazed at Jesus teaching "with authority." What is the difference between someone with genuine authority from God and someone who just has impressive information or charisma?
  • Four friends cut a hole in a roof to lower their mate down to Jesus (2:1-12). Who in your life is that kind of friend for you spiritually? Are you that kind of friend for anyone?
Deeper

The leper says "if you are willing, you can make me clean." Jesus says "I am willing." Many people believe Jesus is able but doubt he is willing. How does that doubt show up in your prayer life, and what does this passage say to it?

Take It Away This Week

  • Memorise Mark 1:15. Pray it back to God each morning this week as a declaration, not just a memory exercise.
  • Read Mark 2:13-3:6 before next week. Jesus is about to become very controversial.

Prayer Focus

Pray boldly for each person to encounter Jesus' authority in a specific area this week. Name those areas out loud. Trust God to move.
Week 3

Jesus the Controversy

Mark 2:13 - 3:35
▼

"It is not the healthy who need a doctor, but the sick. I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners."

Mark 2:17
Jesus eats with tax collectors and sinners, doesn't fast like the Pharisees, picks grain on the Sabbath, heals on the Sabbath, and then his own family thinks he's gone mad. The religious establishment is so threatened they start plotting to kill him. Jesus redraws the lines of who belongs, not by bloodline or behaviour, but by doing the will of God. He is not making religion better; he is replacing it entirely.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Have you ever been judged for who you spent time with? What did that reveal to you about who sets the terms for belonging in God's family?
  • Are the Pharisees all bad people? If they seemingly loved God and knew scripture, why did they miss Jesus so badly? What is the warning to us?
  • Jesus' own family comes to restrain him, thinking "he is out of his mind" (3:21). Why is it sometimes the people closest to us who are most resistant to our obedience to God? How do we stay faithful when that happens?

Activity

Give everyone two minutes to write down: "Who in my life would the Pharisees have been uncomfortable with Jesus spending time with?" Then ask: "Am I actually spending time with those people?" Discuss what barriers exist, and then pray specifically for one person each who is outside your usual circle.

Take It Away This Week

  • Spend time this week with someone outside your usual Christian circle. Ask God to open a door to share his love, and trust him with what happens.
  • Read Mark 4:1-34 ahead of next week's session on parables.

Prayer Focus

Pray for those in your group who feel on the margins of church, family, or belonging. Pray for boldness to cross the lines Jesus crossed. Ask God to give your group a reputation for who you welcome, not who you exclude.
Week 4

The Kingdom Is Like...

Mark 4:1-34
▼

"The kingdom of God is like a mustard seed, which is the smallest of all seeds on earth. Yet when planted, it grows and becomes the largest of all garden plants."

Mark 4:31-32
Jesus teaches in parables: stories that reveal truth to those with ears to hear and conceal it from those who are not genuinely seeking. The Parable of the Sower is the key parable; Jesus says if you don't understand this one, you won't understand the rest. The kingdom of God is not arriving with a bang yet. It is growing quietly, invisibly, inevitably, because God is sovereign over its growth.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: What's your favourite analogy for something that's hard to explain? Why does it work where a direct explanation doesn't?
  • In the Sower parable, four types of soil receive the same seed from the same sower. Which soil best describes where you are with God right now, and what would it take for God to change that?
  • Jesus says the worries of life and deceitfulness of wealth "choke" the word (v.19). What is competing with God's word for first place in your life right now? Name it honestly before God.
Deeper

Jesus teaches in parables so that only those genuinely seeking God will understand. What does that tell us about how God reveals himself, and what does it call us to in how we approach scripture?

Take It Away This Week

  • Identify one thing that is choking God's word in your life. Confess it and ask God specifically to uproot it. Don't just name it; pray over it.
  • Read Mark 4:35-6:6 before next week. It is one of the most dramatic sections in the gospel.

Prayer Focus

Ask God to tend the soil of each person's heart. Pray for the things that are choking the word to be named and surrendered. Trust God to produce fruit in his timing, not ours.
Week 5

Who Is This Man?

Mark 4:35 - 6:6
▼

"Who is this? Even the wind and the waves obey him!"

Mark 4:41
In rapid succession Jesus calms a storm with a word, casts out a legion of demons, heals a woman who has suffered for 12 years, and raises a dead girl. Each miracle is in a different domain: weather, spiritual forces, sickness, death. Nothing is outside his authority. Then he goes to his hometown and can barely do anything there because of unbelief. The question of who Jesus is demands a concrete answer, because your answer determines everything.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Describe a time when you were genuinely afraid. Where did you turn, and was it to God?
  • The disciples have just seen Jesus preach and heal all day, yet in the storm they panic and say "Don't you care if we drown?" (4:38). What does their panic reveal about where their actual trust still was, and where is yours?
  • The woman with the bleeding issue had exhausted every other option before coming to Jesus (5:26). What does her desperation reveal about what genuine trust in God actually requires of us?
Deeper

Jesus tells people not to tell anyone about the miracles. He is building a kingdom that does not run on spectacle or popular momentum. What does that warn us about the kind of faith we build our lives on?

Activity

Take five minutes of silence together. Ask each person to bring before God the storm they are currently in. Then read Mark 4:39 aloud: "Quiet! Be still!" Sit in that for 30 seconds. Then let people share what God said to them, not just what they thought about. The goal is to practise actually listening to God, not just discussing him.

Take It Away This Week

  • Read Mark 6:7-8:21. Watch for the feeding of the 5,000 and what the disciples still don't understand afterwards.
  • Pray for one specific miracle this week. Something you genuinely need God to intervene in. Name it, pray it, and trust him with the outcome.

Prayer Focus

Name the storms in the room and pray over each one specifically. Ask God to move. Don't rush to the next item. Let people be prayed for with time and faith.
Week 6

Still Don't Get It?

Mark 6:7 - 8:21
▼

"Do you still not see or understand? Are your hearts hardened? Do you have eyes but fail to see, and ears but fail to hear?"

Mark 8:17-18
The 12 are sent out with authority. Jesus feeds 5,000 people. He walks on water. He feeds another 4,000. And yet the disciples still don't understand who he is. They worry about bread in the boat when they've just seen him feed thousands from almost nothing. Hardness of heart is a more dangerous problem than most people realise, and it is not only a problem for unbelievers. It can take root in people already following Jesus.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Has there been a time when God showed up clearly in your life but you quickly forgot or explained it away? What happened?
  • After the disciples see Jesus walk on water, Mark says "their hearts were hardened" (6:52). How does a heart become hardened toward God even in the middle of following him? What are the warning signs?
  • Jesus sends the 12 out with "nothing for the journey except a staff" (6:8). How dependent on God are you actually living right now? What are you still managing in your own strength that you haven't handed to him?
Deeper

Jesus warns against the "yeast of the Pharisees and of Herod" (8:15). Yeast works invisibly and gradually. What thinking or attitude is quietly shaping your view of Jesus in ways that don't line up with what Mark shows us?

Take It Away This Week

  • Write down five specific things God has done for you that you never want to forget. Keep it somewhere visible and thank him for each one this week.
  • Read Mark 8:22-9:29 before next week. This is the turning point of the whole gospel.

Prayer Focus

Ask God to soften hearts, including your own. Pray against hardness, cynicism, and spiritual dullness. Ask for genuine wonder at who Jesus is to replace familiarity that has become empty routine.
Week 7

The Turning Point

Mark 8:22 - 9:29
▼

"You are the Messiah." ... "Whoever wants to be my disciple must deny themselves and take up their cross and follow me."

Mark 8:29, 34
This is the hinge of Mark's gospel. Everything before is building to Peter's confession: "You are the Messiah." Everything after flows from what Jesus now reveals: the Messiah must suffer and die. Peter can't handle this and is rebuked sharply. Then the Transfiguration, a breathtaking glimpse of who Jesus really is. Then a father's desperate prayer for his son. The question shifts from "who is Jesus?" to "will you actually follow him on his terms?"

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: What is the hardest thing someone has ever called you to surrender? Did you do it?
  • Peter confesses Jesus as Messiah and then immediately tries to redirect him away from the cross. Why is it so easy to accept Jesus as Saviour but resist him as Lord over specific areas of your life?
  • "Whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life for me and for the gospel will save it" (8:35). What does losing your life for Jesus actually look like for a young adult in Australia right now? Be specific.
Deeper

The father cries out "I do believe; help me overcome my unbelief!" (9:24). This is not a contradiction; it is the most honest prayer in Mark. How does it reshape the way we should come to God when faith is hard?

Take It Away This Week

  • Memorise Mark 8:34-35 this week. Let it sit with you. It is the core call of discipleship in Mark.
  • Read Mark 9:30-10:52 before next week's session.

Prayer Focus

Pray the father's prayer together: "I believe; help my unbelief." Be honest about where faith is hard right now. Ask God for the kind of trust that holds not because life is easy but because he is faithful.
Week 8

Greatness Upside Down

Mark 9:30 - 10:52
▼

"Whoever wants to become great among you must be your servant, and whoever wants to be first must be slave of all."

Mark 10:43-44
As Jesus predicts his death a second time, the disciples argue about who is the greatest. Jesus teaches on welcoming children, on wealth (the rich young ruler), and on servant leadership. Bartimaeus, blind and begging by the roadside, is perhaps the most clear-sighted character in the whole gospel; he refuses to be silenced and calls out to Jesus by his Messianic title. Greatness in God's kingdom looks nothing like the world's version, and it costs everything.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Who is the most genuinely servant-hearted person you know? What marks them out from someone who just performs service?
  • The disciples argue about greatness right after Jesus predicts his death (9:31-34). What does that tell us about how deeply the world's definition of success is still operating in us, even as Christians?
  • The rich young ruler comes to Jesus sincerely and goes away sad. Jesus does not soften the call to make it easier. What does that tell us about the nature of discipleship, and is there an area in your life where you have been walking away from Jesus' call rather than into it?
Deeper

Bartimaeus is told to be quiet by the crowd but shouts louder. The rich young ruler is personally invited by Jesus but walks away. What is the difference between them, and what does it reveal about what God actually responds to?

Activity

Group challenge: Brainstorm five ways your young adults group could genuinely serve someone this month. Not perform, not be seen doing good, but actually lay something down for someone at cost to yourselves. Pick one, plan it concretely, and commit to a date. Servanthood that stays as an idea isn't servanthood.

Take It Away This Week

  • Do one anonymous act of service this week. Tell no one. Let it be between you and God.
  • Read Mark 11-12 before next week. Jesus arrives in Jerusalem and the tension reaches breaking point.

Prayer Focus

Pray against the ambition and comparison that so easily creeps in. Ask God to genuinely reshape your group's idea of greatness so that it looks like Jesus washing feet, not building platforms.
Week 9

Jerusalem, Temple, Truth

Mark 11 - 12
▼

"Love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind and with all your strength... There is no commandment greater than these."

Mark 12:30
Jesus enters Jerusalem on a donkey to cheering crowds, then drives out the traders from the temple in one of his most confronting acts. He curses a fig tree, fields hostile questions from religious leaders about taxes, resurrection, and authority, and watches a poor widow give everything she has. Two chapters of mounting tension as Jesus makes clear that Israel's religious system has missed the point entirely. He is the point.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Is there anything you do in your faith life that has become habit or performance rather than genuine love for God?
  • Jesus drives out the traders because the temple has become "a den of robbers" rather than a house of prayer. What has crowded out genuine prayer and encounter with God in your life, and what would it take to clear it out?
  • Jesus returns Caesar's coin to Caesar and calls us to give God's things to God. What belongs fully to God in your life that you are still managing as though it is yours?
Deeper

The religious leaders know the scriptures better than almost anyone, yet they are the most threatened by Jesus. What does that warn us about the difference between knowing the Bible and actually surrendering to the God it reveals?

Take It Away This Week

  • Read Mark 13-14 before next week. It is Jesus' longest teaching block in Mark, followed immediately by betrayal and arrest.
  • Spend time in prayer this week that is purely about God. Not requests, just worship. Tell him who he is. Let him fill the space.

Prayer Focus

Use the Great Commandment as your prayer framework. Pray through each dimension: heart, soul, mind, strength. Ask God to show each person where their love for him has grown cold and where he wants to reignite it.
Week 10

Watch. Pray. Don't Fall Away.

Mark 13 - 14
▼

"Watch and pray so that you will not fall into temptation. The spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak."

Mark 14:38
Jesus gives his great end-times discourse, then comes the Last Supper, Gethsemane, betrayal, and arrest. Judas betrays him with a kiss. Peter denies him three times after swearing he never would. The gap between good intentions and faithful action is devastating. And at the centre of it all is Jesus in Gethsemane: in agony, in prayer, completely obedient. The contrast between his faith and the disciples' failure is the starkest in the gospel.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Describe a time when you genuinely intended to do the right thing and completely failed to follow through. What did that reveal?
  • In Gethsemane, Jesus asks the disciples to keep watch and they fall asleep three times. What does it look like to spiritually fall asleep in your faith? Are there signs of that in your life right now?
  • Jesus prays "not what I will, but what you will" in Gethsemane (14:36). What is the area of your life where you most struggle to actually mean that prayer when you pray it?
Deeper

Jesus gives extensive teaching in chapter 13 about not being deceived. He takes spiritual deception more seriously than most Christians do today. What are the modern equivalents of the false signs and teachings he warns against, and how do we guard against them?

Activity

Read Mark 14:32-42 slowly and aloud together. Don't rush to application. Sit with the image of Jesus in Gethsemane: alone, in agony, fully obedient. Then ask: what does this tell you about the God you are following? Give people space to respond with what God is actually saying to them, not just what they think about the passage.

Take It Away This Week

  • Read Mark 15 slowly and alone before next week. The crucifixion. Don't rush it.
  • Write your own Gethsemane prayer this week. Be honest about what you are asking God to take. End with "not my will, but yours." Mean it.

Prayer Focus

Pray for each other's faithfulness under pressure. Ask God to build the kind of character in your group that holds when things get hard. Not just good intentions, but genuine rootedness in him that comes from time in prayer.
Week 11

The Cross

Mark 14:53 - 15:47
▼

"My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?"

Mark 15:34
The trial. The flogging. The crown of thorns. Simon of Cyrene carrying the cross. The crucifixion. The mocking. The darkness. The temple curtain torn in two. The centurion's confession. The burial. Mark's account is stark and unflinching; he doesn't soften anything. The most powerful figure in the gospel ends up silent, crucified, dead. And yet this is precisely the point. The cross is not a detour from God's plan; it is the centre of it.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Have you ever had a moment when you felt completely abandoned by people, or by God? Where did you find God in it, looking back?
  • Jesus cries "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" These are the opening words of Psalm 22. He is not losing his faith; he is praying scripture in the darkest moment. What does that call us to when God feels absent?
  • The centurion, a Roman soldier and a pagan, is the first person in Mark to declare Jesus "the Son of God," and he says it at the cross, not at a miracle. What is it about the crucifixion specifically that reveals who Jesus is?
Deeper

Jesus refuses the wine mixed with myrrh (15:23), a pain reliever, and faces the cross fully present. He does not take the edge off. What does that tell us about the nature of his sacrifice, and how does it shape the way we receive what he did?

Activity

Take communion together as a group: simply, informally, with bread and juice. Read Mark 14:22-24 first. Then take it together. Don't over-explain or over-structure it. Let the bread and cup say what they say. Close in prayer that responds to what God has done, not just what we feel.

Take It Away This Week

  • Read Psalm 22 alongside Mark 15. See how closely the crucifixion follows the psalm. Let it deepen your confidence that this was God's plan from the beginning.
  • Come to next week ready to share what the cross has actually done for you and in you, not just what you believe about it theologically.

Prayer Focus

Let prayer be quiet and weighted this week. Thank Jesus specifically, not generically. Tell him what the cross means to you. This session deserves space. Don't rush the ending.
Week 12

He Has Risen. Now Go.

Mark 16
▼

"Don't be alarmed. You are looking for Jesus the Nazarene, who was crucified. He has risen! He is not here."

Mark 16:6
Mark's original ending at 16:8 is abrupt and stunning. The women flee the tomb trembling and afraid and say nothing to anyone. It is an ending that drops the baton at the reader's feet. Will you pick it up? The longer ending (16:9-20) adds resurrection appearances and the Great Commission. Either way, the question Mark closes with is the same one he opened with: what will you do with Jesus?

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: What has God shown you about himself through these 12 weeks in Mark that you did not see as clearly before?
  • The women come to the tomb fully expecting to find a body. They are not looking for a resurrection; they are there to anoint a corpse. How does the resurrection of Jesus call us to completely rebuild our expectations of what God can do?
  • "He has risen! He is not here." If you actually built your life on that fact, not just believed it mentally but staked everything on it, what would change concretely this week?
Deeper

The disciples are commissioned to "go into all the world" (16:15). This is not a suggestion for people with the right personality or gifting. For a young adult in your city, your workplace, your family: what does going actually look like, and what is the one specific step you will take this week?

Take It Away

  • Re-read Mark 1:1 now that you have finished the whole gospel. How does "the beginning of the good news" land differently after 16 chapters?
  • Tell one person outside this group what God has shown you about Jesus over these 12 weeks. Not a sermon. Just a conversation. Trust God with where it goes.

Prayer Focus

Pray for boldness in each person to live as though the resurrection is true, because it is. Thank God for what he has done in this group. Commission each other to go. Ask God to make your group a people who are known not for their meetings but for their witness.
