About This Series

The Fastest, Most Urgent Gospel

Mark is the shortest and most action-packed of the four Gospels. Written likely by John Mark (a companion of both Peter and Paul), it moves at a relentless pace. The word "immediately" appears over 40 times. There's no nativity story, no genealogy — Mark throws us straight into the action.

This is a gospel for people who want to cut to the chase. Who is Jesus? What did he actually do? What does it cost to follow him? Mark doesn't let you sit on the fence.

⚡

Urgency

Mark's "immediately" pace calls us to respond now, not someday.

👑

Authority

Jesus commands demons, storms, sickness and death with equal power.

🎭

Suffering Servant

The Son of God comes not to be served, but to give his life as a ransom.

🚶

Discipleship

Following Jesus is costly, confusing, and utterly worth it.

How to Use This Guide

Getting the Most Out of Each Session

Before the Session

Read the passage yourself during the week. Jot down one thing that struck you before you arrive.

During the Session

Read the passage aloud together first. Use the questions as a guide, not a checklist. Let conversation breathe.

Discussion Questions

Each session has four questions: one opener to get everyone talking, two general discussion questions, and one deeper question to push further. Use them as a guide, not a checklist — let conversation breathe.

Closing Well

Always end with the "Take It Away" challenge and pray specifically for one thing from the passage.

The Study Plan

Week 1

Straight Into It

Mark 1:1-20
▼

"The time has come. The kingdom of God has come near. Repent and believe the good news!"

Mark 1:15
Mark wastes no time. There's no Christmas story, no genealogy. In just 20 verses we get John the Baptist, Jesus' baptism, his temptation in the wilderness, and the call of the first four disciples. This is your series overview session — set the tone by asking: who did you think Jesus was before you became a Christian, versus who you think he is now?

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: If you had to describe Jesus in one word to a friend who'd never heard of him, what would you say?
  • Mark calls this "the beginning of the good news." What makes the gospel actually good news in your life right now?
  • When Jesus calls Simon and Andrew, they drop their nets "immediately." What would it look like if you responded to Jesus with that same urgency this week?
Deeper

John says someone greater than him is coming. When was the last time you were genuinely in awe of who Jesus is?

Activity

Go around the room and each person share: "Before I really understood who Jesus was, I thought he was..." Give everyone 60 seconds. It's a great low-pressure opener and will naturally flow into the discussion.

Take It Away This Week

  • Read Mark 1:21-45 before next session and notice how many miracles happen and how fast.
  • Identify one area of your life where Jesus is calling you to "drop your nets." Write it down.

Prayer Focus

Pray for fresh eyes to see Jesus clearly throughout this series. Ask God to speak through Mark's Gospel specifically to your group.
Week 2

Who Has Authority Like This?

Mark 1:21 – 2:12
▼

"What is this? A new teaching — and with authority! He even gives orders to impure spirits and they obey him."

Mark 1:27
Jesus heals a man with an unclean spirit, cures Simon's mother-in-law, cleanses a leper, and forgives and heals a paralysed man — all in rapid succession. The crowd's question "Who is this?" is the central question of Mark. The religious leaders are already bristling by chapter 2. Jesus claims to forgive sins, which only God can do.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Have you ever witnessed something that you couldn't fully explain — medically, circumstantially, or spiritually? What was it?
  • The crowd is amazed at Jesus teaching "with authority." What's the difference between someone with authority and someone who just has information?
  • Four friends cut a hole in a roof to lower their mate down to Jesus (2:1-12). Who in your life is that kind of friend for you spiritually? Are you that friend for anyone?
Deeper

The leper says "if you are willing, you can make me clean." Jesus says "I am willing." How does knowing Jesus is willing, not just able, change how you approach him in prayer?

Take It Away This Week

  • Memorise Mark 1:15. Say it to yourself when you feel like responding to Jesus can wait.
  • Read Mark 2:13-3:6 — next week we get into controversy territory.

Prayer Focus

Pray for each person to encounter Jesus' authority in a specific area this week. Be bold and specific in your requests together.
Week 3

Jesus the Controversy

Mark 2:13 – 3:35
▼

"It is not the healthy who need a doctor, but the sick. I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners."

Mark 2:17
Jesus eats with tax collectors and sinners, doesn't fast like the Pharisees, picks grain on the Sabbath, heals on the Sabbath, and then his own family thinks he's gone mad. The religious establishment is so threatened they start plotting to kill him. Jesus redraws the lines of who belongs — not by bloodline or behaviour, but by doing the will of God.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Have you ever been judged for who you spent time with? How did it feel?
  • The Pharisees are the religious elite — they're not bad people, they love God. So why do they miss Jesus so badly?
  • Jesus says "whoever does God's will is my brother and sister and mother" (3:35). How does this challenge or encourage you about what it means to belong in God's family?
Deeper

Jesus' family comes to restrain him, thinking "he is out of his mind" (3:21). Why is it sometimes the people closest to us who are most resistant to our faith?

Activity

Give everyone 2 minutes to write down: "Who in my life would the Pharisees have been uncomfortable with Jesus spending time with?" Then: "Am I actually spending time with those people?" Not to preach at them — but to genuinely be present. Discuss what barriers exist and why.

Take It Away This Week

  • Intentionally spend time this week with someone outside your usual Christian circle. Not to evangelise — just to be present and genuinely interested in them.
  • Read Mark 4:1-34 ahead of next week's session on parables.

Prayer Focus

Pray for those in the group who feel on the margins — of church, of family, of belonging. Remind each other that Jesus specifically sought those people out.
Week 4

The Kingdom Is Like...

Mark 4:1-34
▼

"The kingdom of God is like a mustard seed, which is the smallest of all seeds on earth. Yet when planted, it grows and becomes the largest of all garden plants."

Mark 4:31-32
Jesus teaches in parables — stories that reveal truth to those with ears to hear and conceal it from those who aren't seeking. The Parable of the Sower is the key parable; Jesus says if you don't understand this one, you won't understand the rest. The kingdom of God is not arriving with a bang yet — it's growing quietly, invisibly, inevitably.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: What's your favourite metaphor or analogy for something you find hard to explain? Why does it work?
  • In the Sower parable, four types of soil receive the same seed. Which soil do you most identify with in this season of your life, and why?
  • Jesus says the worries of life and deceitfulness of wealth "choke" the word (v.19). Be honest: what are the thorns in your life right now?
Deeper

Why does Jesus teach in parables? What does it tell us about how God chooses to reveal himself?

Take It Away This Week

  • Identify one "thorn" — one thing that's competing with God's word in your life. Name it to God in prayer this week.
  • Read Mark 4:35-6:6 — this is one of the most dramatic sections of the gospel.

Prayer Focus

Ask God to tend the soil of each person's heart. Pray for specific thorns people name. Ask for courage to be honest about what's competing with Jesus in your lives.
Week 5

Who Is This Man?

Mark 4:35 – 6:6
▼

"Who is this? Even the wind and the waves obey him!"

Mark 4:41
In rapid succession: Jesus calms a storm with a word, casts out a legion of demons, heals a woman who's suffered for 12 years, and raises a dead girl. Each miracle is in a different domain — weather, spiritual forces, sickness, death. Nothing is outside his authority. Then he goes to his hometown and can barely do anything there because of their unbelief. This section climaxes the first half of Mark's question: who exactly is Jesus?

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Describe a time when you were genuinely afraid. What did you want most in that moment?
  • The disciples have just seen Jesus preach and heal all day — yet in the storm they panic and say "Don't you care if we drown?" (4:38). Why is it so easy to forget who Jesus is in the middle of our own storms?
  • The woman with the bleeding issue had "suffered a great deal" and was "getting worse" (5:26). She reaches out to Jesus as a last resort. Have you ever come to Jesus like that? What happened?
Deeper

Why do you think Jesus tells people not to tell anyone about the miracles (the "Messianic Secret")? What's he protecting against?

Activity

Take 5 minutes of silence. Ask everyone to think about what "storm" they're currently in. Then, read Mark 4:39 aloud: "Quiet! Be still!" — and sit in that for 30 seconds. Then let people share what came up for them. Sometimes the most powerful thing a group can do is be still together before God.

Take It Away This Week

  • Read Mark 6:7-8:21. Watch for the feeding of the 5,000 and what the disciples still don't understand.
  • Pray for one specific miracle this week — something you genuinely need Jesus to intervene in.

Prayer Focus

Name the storms in the room. Pray over each one specifically. Don't rush this — let people be prayed for with time and care.
Week 6

Still Don't Get It?

Mark 6:7 – 8:21
▼

"Do you still not see or understand? Are your hearts hardened? Do you have eyes but fail to see, and ears but fail to hear?"

Mark 8:17-18
The 12 are sent out with authority. Jesus feeds 5,000 people. He walks on water. He feeds another 4,000. And yet the disciples still don't understand who he is. They worry about bread in the boat when they've just seen him feed thousands from almost nothing. Jesus is patient but clearly frustrated. Blindness and hardness of heart are a bigger obstacle than most people realise — even for people already following Jesus.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Has there been a time when someone explained something to you multiple times and you still didn't get it? What finally made it click?
  • The disciples see Jesus walk on water and Mark says "they were completely amazed, for they had not understood about the loaves; their hearts were hardened" (6:51-52). How can you witness something amazing from God and still be hardened? Have you experienced this?
  • Jesus sending out the 12 with "nothing for the journey except a staff" (6:8) is a radical dependence on God and community. How dependent on God are you actually living right now?
Deeper

The "yeast of the Pharisees and of Herod" (8:15) — what yeast is subtly working through your thinking about Jesus right now?

Take It Away This Week

  • Start a simple "stones of remembrance" list — five things God has done for you that you never want to forget. Review it this week.
  • Read Mark 8:22-9:29 — this is the turning point of the whole gospel.

Prayer Focus

Ask God to soften hearts — including your own. Pray against hardness, cynicism, and spiritual dullness. Pray for genuine wonder at who Jesus is.
Week 7

The Turning Point

Mark 8:22 – 9:29
▼

"You are the Messiah." ... "Whoever wants to be my disciple must deny themselves and take up their cross and follow me."

Mark 8:29, 34
This is the hinge of Mark's gospel. Everything before is building to Peter's confession: "You are the Messiah." Everything after flows from what Jesus now reveals: the Messiah must suffer and die. Peter can't handle this and is rebuked sharply. Then the Transfiguration — a breathtaking glimpse of who Jesus really is. Then a father's desperate cry for his son, and Jesus' response. The question shifts from "who is Jesus?" to "what does it mean to follow him?"

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: What's the hardest thing someone's ever asked you to give up?
  • Peter gets it right ("You are the Messiah") and then immediately gets it wrong ("This shall never happen to you!"). Why is it so easy to accept Jesus as Saviour but resist him as Lord?
  • "Whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life for me and for the gospel will save it" (8:35). What does it actually look like to lose your life for Jesus as a young adult in Australia?
Deeper

The father says "I do believe; help me overcome my unbelief!" (9:24). Do you think this is a contradiction, or is it the most honest and powerful prayer in Mark? Why?

Take It Away This Week

  • Memorise Mark 8:34-35 this week. It's the core call of discipleship.
  • Read Mark 9:30-10:52 for next week's session on servant leadership.

Prayer Focus

Pray the father's prayer together: "I believe; help my unbelief." Be honest about where faith is hard right now. Pray for courage to follow Jesus even when it costs something.
Week 8

Greatness Upside Down

Mark 9:30 – 10:52
▼

"Whoever wants to become great among you must be your servant, and whoever wants to be first must be slave of all."

Mark 10:43-44
As Jesus predicts his death a second time, the disciples argue about who is the greatest. Jesus teaches on welcoming children, on divorce, on wealth (the rich young ruler), and on servant leadership. Bartimaeus, blind and begging, is perhaps the most seeing character in the whole gospel — he refuses to be silenced and calls out to Jesus by his Messianic title. This chapter is a masterclass in what it actually looks like to follow Jesus.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Who is the most genuinely servant-hearted person you know? What makes them that way?
  • The disciples argue about greatness right after Jesus predicts his death (9:31-34). What does this tell us about how deeply the world's model of success is wired into us?
  • The rich young ruler goes away sad because he has great wealth (10:22). Jesus loved him before letting him walk away. What does it mean to love someone enough to let them make the wrong choice?
Deeper

Bartimaeus is told to be quiet by the crowd — but shouts louder. Compare him to the rich young ruler. What's the difference between them, and what does it say about what Jesus responds to?

Activity

Group challenge: Brainstorm five ways your young adults group could serve someone else — not perform, not be seen doing good, but actually lay something down for someone. Pick one and actually plan to do it together in the next two weeks. Servanthood that stays theoretical isn't servanthood.

Take It Away This Week

  • Do one anonymous act of service this week. Tell no one. Let it be between you and God.
  • Read Mark 11-12 for next week — Jesus arrives in Jerusalem and things get intense.

Prayer Focus

Pray against the ambition and comparison that so easily creeps in. Ask God to genuinely reshape your idea of greatness to look like Jesus washing feet.
Week 9

Jerusalem, Temple, Truth

Mark 11 – 12
▼

"'Love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind and with all your strength'... There is no commandment greater than these."

Mark 12:30
Jesus enters Jerusalem on a donkey to cheering crowds, then drives out the traders from the temple in one of his most confronting acts. He curses a fig tree, fields hostile questions from religious leaders about taxes, resurrection, and authority, and watches a poor widow give everything she has. Two chapters of mounting tension as Jesus makes clear that Israel's religious system has missed the point — and that he is the point.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Is there anything you do in your faith life that has become more about performance than actual relationship with God?
  • Jesus overturns the tables in the temple because it has become "a den of robbers" rather than a house of prayer. What tends to crowd out genuine prayer and encounter with God in your life?
  • Jesus gives Caesar's coin back to Caesar and God's things to God. In your life, what belongs to God that you're giving to other things?
Deeper

Why do you think the religious leaders — who know the scriptures better than almost anyone — are the ones most threatened by Jesus? What does that warn us about?

Take It Away This Week

  • Read Mark 13-14. This is Jesus' longest teaching block in Mark, followed by betrayal and arrest.
  • Spend time in prayer this week that's purely about love — not requests. Just telling God who he is to you.

Prayer Focus

Use the Great Commandment as a prayer framework. Pray through each dimension — heart, soul, mind, strength — and ask God where he wants to grow your love for him.
Week 10

Watch. Pray. Don't Fall Away.

Mark 13 – 14
▼

"Watch and pray so that you will not fall into temptation. The spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak."

Mark 14:38
Jesus gives his great end-times discourse, then comes the Last Supper, Gethsemane, betrayal, and arrest. Judas betrays him with a kiss. Peter denies him three times after saying he never would. The gap between intention and action is devastating. Gethsemane is one of the most profound and human moments in all of scripture — Jesus, in agony, asking his Father if there is another way.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Describe a time when you meant to do something good but completely failed to follow through.
  • In Gethsemane, Jesus asks the disciples to "keep watch" — and they fall asleep three times. What does it look like to spiritually "fall asleep" in your faith? Are there signs of that in your life right now?
  • Jesus prays "not what I will, but what you will" in Gethsemane (14:36). What is the area of your life where you most struggle to pray that prayer and mean it?
Deeper

Why does Jesus give so much teaching in chapter 13 about not being deceived? What are the modern equivalents of the false prophets and signs he warns about?

Activity

Sit together and read Mark 14:32-42 slowly and aloud. Don't rush to application. Just sit with the image of Jesus in Gethsemane — alone, in agony, completely obedient. What does this make you feel? What does it tell you about God? Give people space to respond honestly.

Take It Away This Week

  • Read Mark 15 — the crucifixion — slowly and alone before next week. Don't rush it.
  • Write your own Gethsemane prayer this week. What are you asking God to take? Can you end with "not my will, but yours"?

Prayer Focus

Pray for each other's faithfulness under pressure. Ask God to build the kind of character that holds when things get hard — not just good intentions, but real rootedness in him.
Week 11

The Cross

Mark 14:53 – 15:47
▼

"My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?"

Mark 15:34
The trial. The flogging. The crown of thorns. Simon of Cyrene carrying the cross. The crucifixion. The mocking. The darkness. The temple curtain torn in two. The centurion's confession. The burial. Mark's account is stark and unflinching — he doesn't soften anything. The most powerful figure in the gospel ends up silent, crucified, dead. And yet somehow, this is the point of everything.

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: Have you ever had a moment when you felt completely abandoned — by people, or by God? How did you get through it?
  • Jesus cries "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" — words from Psalm 22. Does knowing he was quoting scripture change how you hear it, or make it more raw, not less?
  • The centurion — a Roman soldier, a pagan — is the first human to declare Jesus "the Son of God" after seeing how he died (15:39). What is it about the cross specifically that reveals who Jesus is?
Deeper

Jesus refuses the wine mixed with myrrh (15:23) — a pain reliever. He chose to face the cross fully conscious and present. What does that tell us about the nature of his sacrifice?

Activity

This week, consider doing communion together as a group — simply, informally, with bread and juice. Read Mark 14:22-24 first. Then take it together. Don't over-explain or over-structure it. Let the simplicity of bread and cup do the work.

Take It Away This Week

  • Read Psalm 22 this week alongside Mark 15. Watch how closely Jesus' crucifixion follows the psalm.
  • Read Mark 16 — the final session. Come ready to share what the cross means to you personally, not theologically.

Prayer Focus

Let prayer be quiet and weighted this week. Thank Jesus specifically — not generically. Tell him what the cross means to you. This session deserves space, not a rushed ending.
Week 12

He Has Risen. Now Go.

Mark 16
▼

"Don't be alarmed. You are looking for Jesus the Nazarene, who was crucified. He has risen! He is not here."

Mark 16:6
Mark's original ending at 16:8 is abrupt and stunning — the women flee the tomb trembling and afraid and "said nothing to anyone." It's an ending that puts the ball in the reader's court. Will you be afraid and silent, or will you go and tell? The longer ending (16:9-20) adds appearances and the Great Commission. Either way, the question Mark leaves you with is the same one he started with: what will you do with Jesus?

Discussion Questions

  • Opener: What has changed for you personally over these 12 weeks? What's one thing you think or feel differently about?
  • The women come to the tomb expecting to find a body — they come to anoint a corpse. What do you come to Jesus expecting? Does the resurrection change those expectations?
  • "He has risen! He is not here." If you genuinely believed that with everything in you, what would change in how you live this week?
Deeper

The disciples are commissioned to "go into all the world" (16:15). For a young adult in your city, your workplace, your family — what does "going" actually look like? What's one specific step you can take?

Take It Away

  • Re-read Mark 1:1 now that you've finished the whole gospel. How does "the beginning of the good news" land differently after 16 chapters?
  • Tell one person who isn't in this group what you've learned about Jesus over these 12 weeks. Don't make it a sermon — just share it like a conversation.

Prayer Focus

Pray for boldness to be the opposite of silent. Thank God for what he's done in this group. Ask for courage to live as resurrection people — not just believe it on paper, but live like it's actually true.
